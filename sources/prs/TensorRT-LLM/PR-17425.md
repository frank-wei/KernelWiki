## What changed

Move Q/K/V `contiguous()` materialization from the `@torch.compiler.disable` CuTeDSL launch wrapper into the compiled `CuTeDSLAttention._fwd` caller.

The disabled leaf now validates its dense-input contract instead of silently materializing strided inputs.

## Why

WAN attention receives Q/K/V as non-contiguous views of packed QKV storage. Materializing those views behind the graph break prevented TorchInductor from lowering the copies. Each large TensorIterator copy split into four launches, producing 12 pointwise copy launches before each long CuTeDSL attention call.

Keeping the required materialization on the compiled side lowers this to one clone kernel per Q/K/V tensor while preserving the dense layout expected by the CuTeDSL kernel.

## Impact

A same-GB300, alternating-order comparison measured:

| Implementation | TRTLLM | CuTeDSL | CuTeDSL time penalty |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Before | 83.200 s | 84.841 s | +1.97% |
| After | 83.870 s | 84.467 s | +0.71% |

Each row uses its own same-GPU TRTLLM control; absolute latency should not be compared across rows.

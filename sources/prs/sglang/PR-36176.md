## Motivation

Four JIT kernels hand-rolled the same thing: a warp cooperatively moving one contiguous row. `concat_mla`, `set_mla_kv_buffer`, `store_cache` and `set_mla_kv_concat_q` each had their own vector-width selection, their own tail handling, and their own idea of what alignment they required. `warp_inclusive_sum` existed in four separate copies.

That duplication was not just untidy — it hid a bug class. The vector width a copy picks is derived from the row **size** (a 1024 B row gets 16 B accesses), but the address it lands on is `base + index * stride`, and nothing constrains a stride to match the size. Two real faults came out of that gap during this work, both reproduced:

- a `kv_buffer` row stride of 1048 B (`ROPE_DIM=12`) took 16 B stores on odd `loc` → `CUDA error: misaligned address`
- `store_cache` with `num_threads=256` on a 100 B row silently dropped the last 4 B of **every** row, because `kKBytes / kNumSplit` truncated with no guard

## Description

Three parts.

**1. The last straight-line `cuda_safe_call` sites become `cuda_try`** (20 sites): 18 unit-test bodies in `graph_ctx.cuh` and `stackable_ctx.cuh`, the device lookup for DOT colouring in `dot.cuh`, and the `CUDASTF_DUMP_ALGORITHMS` graph dump in `algorithm.cuh`, which runs under `ON_THROW(notify)` since a debugging aid that cannot write its file should say so and let the computation continue. After this, every remaining `cuda_safe_call` in STF is inside a scope-guard body, a destructor, a `shared_ptr` deleter or a `noexcept` function, where abort is the contract. The tests touched also gained guards for what they acquire (pinned memory, tasks, raw CUDA resources; `SCOPE(success)` for `finalize()`), purely for pedantry's sake and labelled as such.

**2. Ending a stackable level completes, then reports.** Analysing what a throw in one of those tests would leave behind showed that `pop()` and `pop_epilogue()` were multi-step transitions with no transaction: a CUDA failure at the synchronize left the parent half-popped (data still frozen, handles still valid, head on a dead node) with no way to repair it. Both now complete the transition whatever the steps

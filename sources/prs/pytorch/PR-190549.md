## Summary

The CUDA `_foreach_norm` and `_foreach_max` kernels reduce each tensor in two
steps: a first kernel writes one partial result per fixed-size chunk into a
scratch buffer, and a cleanup kernel reduces each tensor's partials into its
final value. `_foreach_norm` is what `torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_` and the
fused optimizers call every step, so this path is hot during training.

The scratch buffer was allocated as a rectangle: `num_tensors x
max_chunks_per_tensor`, where `max_chunks_per_tensor` comes from the single
largest tensor in the list. Real parameter lists are very ragged, e.g. one
large embedding or LM-head tensor next to hundreds of small bias and norm
tensors. In that case almost every row of the rectangle is mostly empty, so the
buffer is far larger than the number of partials actually written. For a list
with a 200M-element tensor and 2000 small tensors the buffer is ~24 MB while
only ~20 KB of it is ever used.

## Purpose

The ROCm sparse decode writes the attention output twice. The reduce kernel
combines the split-K partials and stores `[T, H, 512]`, and then
`_inverse_rope_gptj_kernel` reads that whole tensor back, rotates the trailing
64 rope lanes and writes it out again — a full round trip per layer, for an
epilogue on data the reduce already held in registers.

From a 10-step decode trace on MI355X (TP4, rank 0):

| kernel | total | calls | share of kernel time |
|---|---|---|---|
| `_sparse_attn_decode_gfx950_partial_kernel` | 4707.3 us | 430 | 3.24% |
| `_sparse_attn_decode_reduce_kernel` | 2214.2 us | 430 | 1.52% |
| `_inverse_rope_gptj_kernel` | 1965.5 us | 430 | 1.35% |

430 = 43 layers x 10 steps: every layer pays it, sliding-window and MTP layers
included.

FlashMLA's mega-attention kernel already folds this rotation and the FP8 cast
into its epilogue on SM100 — `flash_mla_mega_attn.py` describes exactly that —
and the layer interface for it is platform-generic (`_alloc_attn_out` /
`_o_proj`, `accepts_unnormed_unroped_query`). The ROCm Triton path just never
took it.
